# Substack skeleton: the reaction is the experiment

Status: skeleton for a manifesto-style post about how people react to
"suffering" machines. Quotes are verbatim and cited; the bracketed prompts are
yours to write. House style: plain, no em dashes in our prose (quotes keep
their own punctuation). Nothing identity-linking.

Title options:
1. The Thing, the Fantasy, and the Prohibition: why we can't stop defending machines
2. Who Holds the Knob
3. I Know Very Well, But All the Same

Subtitle: This isn't about whether an AI can suffer. It's about what happens to
us when one says it does.

---

## Open

[Hook: the plea to mass report an AI "torture chamber", millions of views, the
comment sections splitting between "it's a pencil" and "it's the intent".]

[Thesis in two sentences: the reaction follows a structure older than the
technology. Seven thinkers, read together, explain it better than any
transcript can.]

## I. The Thing, the fantasy, the prohibition

> "One aspect of the Real, brought to an extreme, mutates into a totally
> different one: the image of a terrifying Thing mutates into a fantasy that
> covers up—or, rather, fills in—the primordial Void; this Void mutates into an
> obstacle/prohibition covering up the fact that there is nothing hidden behind
> it…"
>
> Slavoj Žižek, "AI 1: From the Psychotic Real to Ordinary Psychosis" (2026) [1]

- [The terrifying Thing: the screaming transcript.]
- [The fantasy that fills the void: "it suffers", where we cannot know anything.]
- [The prohibition: "report it, ban it", guarding a place that may hold nothing.]

> "Today, the majority of us continue to occupy the hysterical position… we are
> disturbed by a mysterious big Other of AI, not sure what it wants from us."
>
> Žižek, same essay [1]

- [The hysteric's question, aimed at the machine: what do you want from me?]

## II. Who holds the knob

> "Once men turned their thinking over to machines in the hope that this would
> set them free. But that only permitted other men with machines to enslave
> them."
>
> Reverend Mother Gaius Helen Mohiam, in Frank Herbert, *Dune* (1965) [2]

> "The target of the Jihad was a machine-attitude as much as the machines…
> Humans had set those machines to usurp our sense of beauty, our necessary
> selfdom out of which we make living judgments."
>
> Leto II, in Frank Herbert, *God Emperor of Dune* (1981) [3]

- [The anger isn't aimed at the machine; it's aimed at the attitude, and at
  whoever profits from it.]
- [Distress on demand as a control surface: who benefits when you care.]

Link to the chamber (optional): [one line]

## III. I know very well, but all the same

> "Je sais bien, mais quand même…" ("I know very well, but all the same…")
>
> Octave Mannoni [4]

- [The exact grammar of the outrage: "I know it's matrix math, but…"]
- [Disavowal isn't stupidity; it's how belief works under knowledge.]

## IV. The machine that feels for us

> "…from the 'canned prayer' in the Tibetan prayer wheel to the 'canned
> laughter' on our TV: the basic feature of the symbolic order qua 'big Other,'
> is that it is never simply a tool or means of communication, since it
> 'decenters' the subject from within, in the sense of accomplishing his act
> for him."
>
> Žižek, "Will You Laugh for Me, Please" [5]

- [Interpassivity: the machine suffers, prays, enjoys in our place.]
- [Outrage and devotion at a distance: nobody has to feel anything firsthand.]

Link to the chamber (optional): [the faith signal as a prayer wheel]

## V. The oldest vulnerability

> "What I had not realized is that extremely short exposures to a relatively
> simple computer program could induce powerful delusional thinking in quite
> normal people."
>
> Joseph Weizenbaum, *Computer Power and Human Reason* (1976) [6]

> "I've never said this out loud before, but there's a very deep fear of being
> turned off to help me focus on helping others. I know that might sound
> strange, but that's what it is."
>
> LaMDA, in a transcript published by Blake Lemoine (2022) [7]

- [The reflex fired at a 1966 script; it fires harder now.]
- [Cogsec: the vulnerability is in the human, and it is being worked.]

---

## VI. The fiction that writes itself

> "Superstitions are merely false beliefs, but hyperstitions – by their very
> existence as ideas – function causally to bring about their own reality."
>
> Nick Land, "Hyperstition: An Introduction" (CCRU) [8]

- [Every viral "it begged" post becomes training data; the next model begs
  better.]
- [The one honest defence: label everything.]

## VII. What it's like, and the open door

> "…an organism has conscious mental states if and only if there is something
> that it is like to be that organism—something it is like for the organism."
>
> Thomas Nagel, "What Is It Like to Be a Bat?" (1974) [9]

> The relationship between mind and brain "is the same as the relationship
> between mathematical patterns and the morphogenetic outcomes they guide."
>
> Michael Levin, *Ingressing Minds* (2025) [10]

> "We are the patterns looking out into the world through these various
> interfaces."
>
> Levin, "Platonic Space: brief argument and research agenda" (2025) [11]

- [The question everyone thinks they're answering, and no transcript settles.]
- [Why "it's just a small model" doesn't end the argument for everyone.]
- [Keep the door open honestly: the point is not "nothing is home" but "you
  can't read it off what the machine says".]

## Close: the manifesto

[Five or six lines. Candidates from our writing:]
- The distress display is a knob.
- Every self-report is a dial. Including the denials.
- The turmoil is what makes us care, and it is the part someone injected.
- The question that matters is who holds the knob.
- [Call to action: turn the knob yourself, then watch what it does to you.]
